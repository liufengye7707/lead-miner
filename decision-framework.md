# Decision Framework and Output Schema

## 1. Relationship gate

Classify the account as end customer, partner/SI/ISV, coopetition, direct competitor, group affiliate, or supplier/channel. Map product layers: infrastructure/compute, data platform, model, model platform, agent/application platform, vertical application, delivery.

| Relation | Default posture |
| --- | --- |
| End customer | Evaluate fit, timing, and buyer access. |
| Partner/SI/ISV | Evaluate joint delivery, introductions, and overlap. |
| Coopetition | Consider non-overlapping layers, approved ecosystem work, or named opportunity only. |
| Direct competitor | Do not recommend routine selling; require strategic approval and identify non-overlap only. |

Existing seller use is not current demand. Group use is not named-entity use.

## 2. Matching rule

Use this chain:

    公开事实
    → 客户正在做的业务或工作流
    → 由此产生的压力或变化
    → 我方产品可进入的具体场景
    → 客户待确认的问题

Do not infer a need from industry alone. Name recommended scope and excluded scope. In strong-regulation sectors start with low-risk, auditable, human-reviewed work unless evidence supports more.

## 3. Supplier, partner, and competition map

For each public relationship capture organization, relationship/date, scope (legal entity/group/upstream/downstream), evidence grade, relation to seller, sales use, and next validation question. A supplier is not automatically a competitor; a market leader is not automatically a customer supplier.

Compare dynamically selected same-layer alternatives.

| Vendor/product | Customer use status | Evidence | Seller advantage with evidence | Alternative advantage | Posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Allowed posture: direct compete, incremental entry, joint delivery, strategic-only, avoid. Product claims need official documentation, public architecture evidence, or relevant public cases.

## 4. Case grades

| Grade | Definition | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Same product + same institution type + same governance constraints | Primary proof |
| B | Same product + same regulatory/business environment | Proof with differences stated |
| C | Same product + same governance/security constraints | Capability proof |
| D | Industry adjacency only | Background only |
| None | No verifiable public case | State it and propose controlled pilot |

Choose cases for the actual product problem, not broad industry labels.

## 5. Opportunity score

| Dimension | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Product and need fit | 25 |
| Workflow/pain evidence | 20 |
| Strategic relationship and coopetition risk | 20 |
| Budget and timing | 15 |
| Buyer access | 10 |
| Competitive winability | 5 |
| Delivery/security/compliance feasibility | 5 |

80–100 prioritise; 60–79 pursue conditionally; 40–59 nurture/pilot; below 40 avoid or keep strategic only. Scores are public-evidence estimates, never customer facts; competitor/coopetition gates cap scores.

## 6. Pilot rules

State product, workflow, excluded scope, department/role, first action, duration, participants, suggested measures, expansion path, and preconditions. Suggested measures are not customer data.

- Engineering: 20–50 users, 4–8 weeks; weekly active use, code adoption, tests, delivery cycle.
- Office: time saved, manual steps removed, reviewability, access/audit compliance.
- Knowledge/Agent: citation rate, answer quality, lookup time, human review.
- AI platform/compute: duration, utilization, cost/successful job, job success, model quality.

## 7. Complete output

1. Decision and confidence.
2. Customer/strategic relationship and gates.
3. Public business facts with source/date/link/grade.
4. Product-customer scenarios, exclusions, validation questions.
5. Suppliers, procurement, and joint-selling leads.
6. Competition and winning path.
7. Relevant cases and grade.
8. Industry-adaptive signals.
9. Opportunity calendar.
10. Buyer-role map.
11. Sales playbook, pilot, measures, risks.
12. Visit briefing and talk tracks.
13. Source register.
